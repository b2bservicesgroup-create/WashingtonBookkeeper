*Tuesdays are bookkeeping day at B2B Solutions. Each week we share one practical tip to help small business owners keep their books clean, accurate and stress-free.*

A good bookkeeper can reconcile your accounts, categorize your transactions and close out your year. What we can't do is guess. Some of what your books need only lives in your head, your truck, or your email.

Year-end goes fastest when we get these things from you early. Here's what we ask our clients for every November and December.

## 1. Answers to our open questions

Every business has a few transactions we can't identify on our own: a Venmo payment, a check with no memo, a charge from a vendor we've never seen. We'll send you a short list. The sooner you answer it, the sooner those dollars land in the right place instead of sitting in "Uncategorized," where they might cost you a deduction.

## 2. Statements for accounts we don't see

If we don't know an account exists, it can't be in your books. Let us know about:

- **New accounts** you opened this year (a second checking account, a new credit card, a line of credit)
- **Closed accounts**, so we can confirm the final balance
- **Payment apps** like PayPal, Square, Stripe or Venmo that you use for business
- **Personal cards or accounts** you used for business purchases, even once

## 3. Year-end loan statements

When your lender sends the December statement for a business loan, vehicle loan or equipment financing, forward it to us. We use it to confirm the balance in your books matches what you actually owe and to split your payments correctly between principal and interest.

## 4. Receipts for big purchases

Bought a truck, trailer, computer, tools or equipment this year? Send us the purchase agreement or receipt. We need the date, the price and what it was, and your tax preparer will need the same details to handle depreciation.

## 5. Your contractor information

If you paid anyone who isn't an employee (a sub, a cleaner, a web designer), let us know who they are and send us any W-9s you collected. If you don't have a W-9 for someone, request one now. 1099s for 2026 are due February 1, 2027, and chasing down tax ID numbers in late January is no fun for anyone.

## 6. Cash and anything that didn't touch the bank

Cash sales, cash expenses, tips, barter, or a customer who paid you with something other than money: if it didn't go through a bank account, we won't see it unless you tell us. A simple list with dates and amounts is enough.

## 7. Invoices you don't expect to collect

If a customer isn't going to pay, tell us. We'll work with you and your tax preparer on how to handle it so your books don't show income you'll never receive.

## 8. A heads-up on what's changing

Planning to buy equipment before December 31, hire your first employee, add a partner, or close a location? Tell us before it happens, not after. A quick email now saves cleanup later.

## When and where to send it

Aim to get everything to us by **mid-January**. That gives us time to close December and finish your year-end reports ahead of your Department of Revenue filing and your tax preparer's deadlines.

Current clients: send documents and answers to [client@b2bsolutions.biz](mailto:client@b2bsolutions.biz).

## This week's tip

Make a "Year-End 2026" folder in your email today. Every time a loan statement, receipt or W-9 shows up between now and January, drop it in. When it's time, you'll have everything in one place.

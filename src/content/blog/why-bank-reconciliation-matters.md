Your bank balance is not the same as your profit, and your bookkeeping software isn't automatically right just because it's connected to your bank. That's where reconciliation comes in.

## What reconciliation is

Reconciliation means comparing every transaction in your books to your bank and credit card statements and confirming they match, down to the penny. It's how you know your numbers are real.

## What it catches

- **Duplicate transactions** that bank feeds sometimes import twice
- **Missing transactions**, like a cash deposit or check that never got recorded
- **Bank errors and unauthorized charges**, which you only have a limited window to dispute
- **Forgotten subscriptions** still quietly billing you every month
- **Uncleared checks** that make your balance look bigger than it really is

## Why it matters

Unreconciled books lead to bad decisions. You might think you can afford a new hire or a big purchase when you can't. At tax time, errors can mean overpaying or underreporting, and fixing a full year of mismatches in April costs far more than catching them each month.

For Washington businesses, accurate revenue matters even more. B&O tax is charged on gross receipts, so if deposits are missing or doubled in your books, what you report to the Department of Revenue may be off too.

Lenders and investors also look for reconciled financials. Clean books make it easier to get a loan or line of credit when you need one.

## How often to do it

At minimum, monthly, as soon as your statement closes. Busy businesses benefit from reconciling weekly. Either way, don't let it slide: the longer you wait, the harder the problems are to trace.

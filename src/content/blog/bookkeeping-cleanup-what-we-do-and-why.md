If you've opened your accounting software lately and felt your stomach drop, you're not alone. Months of unreconciled transactions, a credit card nobody connected, a loan that's been booked as an expense, a pile of receipts in the truck. It happens to good businesses all the time, usually because the owner was busy doing the actual work.

A bookkeeping cleanup is how we get you from "I have no idea where we stand" to books you can trust. Here's what that involves, and why it's worth doing before tax season forces the issue.

## What is a bookkeeping cleanup?

A cleanup (sometimes called catch-up bookkeeping) is a one-time project that brings your books current and correct. It's different from monthly bookkeeping:

- **Catch-up** means recording months, or even years, of transactions that were never entered.
- **Cleanup** means fixing books that *were* kept but kept wrong: miscategorized expenses, duplicate entries, accounts that never reconciled.

Most businesses need a little of both. When we're done, your books match your bank and card statements, every transaction is in the right category, and your reports actually mean something.

## What we do, step by step

### 1. Assess where things stand

We start by looking at what you have: your accounting software (QuickBooks Online, Xero, Wave or FreshBooks), your bank and credit card statements, loan statements, and any past tax returns. We figure out how far back the problems go and which accounts are affected, so we can scope the project before any work begins.

### 2. Gather the missing pieces

We'll give you a short, specific list of what we need: statements for missing months, loan documents, and answers to questions like "what was this $2,400 payment?" We keep the list as short as possible and handle everything we can find on our own.

### 3. Clean up the chart of accounts

Your chart of accounts is the list of categories every transaction gets sorted into. Over time it tends to collect duplicates, vague catch-all categories and accounts nobody uses. We consolidate and organize it so your profit & loss statement reads clearly and your tax preparer can find what they need.

### 4. Record and categorize every transaction

We bring in every missing transaction and recategorize the ones that landed in the wrong place. This is where common problems get fixed:

- Personal expenses paid from the business account, recorded as owner's draws instead of business expenses
- Loan payments split correctly between principal and interest, instead of booked entirely as an expense
- Transfers between your own accounts recorded as transfers, not income or expenses
- Duplicate entries from bank feeds and manual entries overlapping

### 5. Reconcile every account, month by month

Reconciliation means matching your books to your bank and credit card statements so every dollar is accounted for. We reconcile each account, each month, until the ending balance in your books matches the statement exactly. This is the step that turns "probably right" into "verified."

### 6. Tidy up what you're owed and what you owe

If you invoice customers or track bills, we review open invoices and unpaid bills. Old invoices that were actually paid get matched to their deposits; bills that were paid get cleared. You end up with an accurate list of who really owes you money and what you really owe.

### 7. Check your books against what you reported to Washington

Washington businesses report gross receipts to the Department of Revenue for B&O tax, and many also collect and report sales tax. Once your books are clean, we compare the revenue in your books to what was reported on your excise tax returns. If something doesn't line up, we flag it so you and your tax professional can decide how to handle it.

### 8. Hand off clean books, and keep them that way

You get a clean profit & loss statement and balance sheet for the cleanup period, plus a short summary of what we found and fixed. If you work with a CPA or tax preparer, we can coordinate with them directly. From there, most clients move onto a [monthly bookkeeping plan](/pricing) so the books stay current and never need another cleanup.

## Why it matters

### You can't make good decisions with bad numbers

Messy books can make a profitable business look like it's losing money, or the reverse. Until your accounts are reconciled, you don't really know your margins, your cash position or whether you can afford that next hire or truck.

### Tax season gets cheaper and calmer

When your books are a mess, your tax preparer has to sort it out at their rates, under deadline pressure. Clean, categorized books mean less time billed, fewer missed deductions, and fewer "can you find this?" emails in March.

### Washington taxes run on gross receipts

Washington has no state income tax, but B&O tax is charged on gross receipts, not profit. If your revenue isn't recorded accurately, what you report to the Department of Revenue may not be accurate either. Clean books make your excise returns straightforward and defensible.

### Lenders, landlords and buyers will ask

Applying for a loan or line of credit, signing a commercial lease, or selling the business all start with the same request: "Send us your financial statements." Current, reconciled books let you say yes to opportunities without a scramble.

### Your LLC protection depends on separation

Mixing personal and business money doesn't just muddy your numbers. It can weaken the liability protection your LLC or corporation is meant to provide. A cleanup draws a clear line between business and personal activity, and monthly bookkeeping keeps it there.

### It's easier to stay current than to catch up

Every month you wait adds another month to reconcile, and bank and card records get harder to pull the further back you go. The sooner you start, the smaller and faster the project.

## Signs you need a cleanup

- You haven't reconciled your bank or credit card accounts in months
- Your accounting software shows a balance that doesn't match your bank
- There's a large "Uncategorized" or "Ask My Accountant" balance
- Your tax preparer sent your books back with questions, or charged extra to fix them
- You're not sure whether a loan, vehicle or equipment purchase was recorded correctly
- You switched bookkeepers or software and things didn't carry over cleanly

*Tuesdays are bookkeeping day at B2B Solutions. Each week we share one practical tip to help small business owners keep their books clean, accurate and stress-free.*

There are about seven weeks left in the year. That's plenty of time to close out 2026 cleanly, but only if you start now. Wait until January and you'll be cleaning up the year at the same time you're trying to file for it.

Here's the checklist we work through with our own clients every November and December.

## 1. Get every account reconciled through November

Before you can close the year, your books need to match your bank and credit card statements. Reconcile every account (checking, savings, every credit card, PayPal, Square, Stripe) through the end of November now. That way December is the only month left to finish in January. (New to reconciliation? Start with [why it matters](/blog/why-bank-reconciliation-matters).)

## 2. Clear out "Uncategorized" and "Ask My Accountant"

Most bookkeeping software has a catch-all account where unclear transactions end up. By year-end, that account should be empty. Every dollar sitting there is either a deduction you might miss or income that may be reported wrong.

Go through each item and ask: what was this, and what was it for? If you can't remember, check the receipt, the vendor's website or your email.

## 3. Match your revenue to what you reported to the Department of Revenue

This one is specific to Washington. B&O tax is based on gross receipts, so the income in your books should line up with the income you reported on your excise tax returns this year.

Run a profit and loss report by month and compare it to your filed returns. If something doesn't match, find out why now. It's much easier to amend one return in December than to explain a whole year of differences later.

## 4. Review loans, equipment and owner draws

These are the transactions we see booked wrong most often:

- **Loan payments** shouldn't all be expenses. The principal reduces the loan balance; only the interest is an expense. Compare your loan balance in the books to your lender's year-end statement.
- **Equipment, vehicles and tools** over your capitalization threshold usually belong on the balance sheet, not in supplies. Your tax preparer will want a list with dates and amounts.
- **Owner draws and personal spending** should be recorded as draws, not business expenses. Mixed-up personal and business spending is one of the biggest red flags in an audit.

## 5. Collect W-9s from your contractors now

If you paid an individual or an unincorporated business $600 or more for services this year, you'll likely need to send them a 1099-NEC. The filing deadline is January 31, which falls on a Sunday in 2027, so forms are due Monday, February 1.

Don't wait until late January to chase down tax ID numbers. Pull a report of everyone you paid this year, flag the contractors, and request a W-9 from anyone you don't have one for.

## 6. Collect your unpaid invoices (and write off the ones you won't)

Look at your accounts receivable aging report. Follow up on anything past due before the holidays. For invoices you truly won't collect, talk to your tax preparer about writing them off so your books don't show income you'll never see.

## 7. Make your year-end moves on purpose

If you're planning to buy equipment, prepay expenses or pay bonuses before December 31 for tax reasons, talk with your tax preparer first. Those decisions should be based on accurate numbers, which is exactly why steps 1 through 6 come first.

## Washington deadlines coming up

Mark these on your calendar now (dates moved to the next business day when they fall on a weekend):

| Who | What | Due |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly DOR filers | December 2026 excise tax return | January 25, 2027 |
| Quarterly DOR filers | Q4 2026 excise tax return | February 1, 2027 |
| Annual DOR filers | 2026 annual excise tax return | April 15, 2027 |
| Businesses that paid contractors | 1099-NEC forms to contractors and the IRS | February 1, 2027 |

Not sure which filing frequency you are? It's in the welcome letter the Department of Revenue sent when you registered, and in your My DOR account.

## This week's tip

Block one hour on your calendar this week just for steps 1 and 2. Reconciling and clearing out uncategorized transactions makes everything else on this list faster.
